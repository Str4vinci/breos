# 0.7.x tariffs and smart-charging plan

## Status and decision

This note defines the tariff, smart-charging, economics, and related nonthermal
work proposed for BREOS 0.7.x. It uses a historical research implementation as
design evidence, not as an architecture to copy wholesale.

The recommended 0.7.0 scope is:

1. time-of-use (TOU) tariff valuation and explicit currency;
2. a dispatch-strategy seam in the canonical BREOS battery simulation;
3. deterministic fixed-target smart charging as the supported first strategy;
4. causal daily-persistence smart charging behind an experimental label;
5. real-price/component cashflows and unambiguous payback reporting; and
6. a parity-proven Numba day kernel for repeated objective evaluation, if it
   clears an explicit benchmark gate; and
7. validation oracles for smart-charging schedules, kept outside the public
   runtime API.

Existing local implementation branches whose names still contain `0.6.x`
belong to this 0.7 plan. Do not merge them into the 0.6 release. Rename or
recreate them from the post-0.6 `develop` branch when tariff work resumes.

This schedules the fixed-target part of price-aware dispatch with tariff
valuation in 0.7.0 because the two are most useful when designed together.
Greedy self-consumption remains the default and must retain exact
compatibility. More general predictive optimisation remains later work.

ACC is a worthwhile 0.7.x follow-up, with fixed and proportional allocation a
possible 0.7.0 stretch goal. It should not block the tariff/smart-charging
release because participant settlement adds a second public data model and the
legacy ACC sizer is not production-ready.

**Hard exclusion:** do not port thermal energy storage, heat pumps,
electro-thermal dispatch, A3 thermal calibration, thermal profiles, or their
economics/configuration/reporting fields. Adjacent code in a mixed legacy file
does not weaken this boundary.

## What must not be transplanted

`pvbat/tou.py` contains both useful tariff definitions and an entire parallel
battery simulation. `pvbat/tou_optimizer.py` adds another large execution,
caching, optimisation, revaluation, and reporting stack. Copying either module
as a unit would recreate the architectural duplication that the BREOS facade
and runner are intended to prevent.

In particular, do not port:

- `_simulate_tou_dispatch_arrays()` or either
  `simulate_energy_balance_tou*()` function as a second physics engine;
- tariff-specific battery degradation or replacement loops;
- legacy `run_simulation.py` mode branching;
- silent fallbacks from unknown tariffs to a Portuguese schedule;
- a generic German tariff inferred from an aggregate of distribution-system
  operators;
- the legacy ACC sizer, its internal weather fetch, or its simplified capex and
  module-size assumptions;
- any whole-project or whole-degradation Numba simulation;
- EUR-specific names as the new canonical result contract; or
- thermal fields found in shared legacy economics, optimisation, plotting, or
  configuration modules.

The useful concepts should be re-expressed behind `breos.App`, the declarative
configuration registry, the existing runner, and the canonical battery ledger.

## Target architecture

### Tariffs are a value domain, not a simulation mode

Add a small tariff domain, tentatively `breos/tariffs.py`, with three distinct
concepts:

1. **Schedule** — maps timezone-aware instants to named tariff periods.
2. **Prices** — maps periods to import and export prices in one declared
   currency, with optional standing/fixed charges.
3. **Resolved tariff** — immutable per-timestep import/export price arrays plus
   provenance suitable for valuation and dispatch.

Static presets should live in a packaged resource such as
`breos/data/configs/tariffs.json` and be loaded through `breos.resources`.
Schedule definitions and price vintages must be separable: regulated period
boundaries may remain valid while a supplier's prices change.

The resolver must:

- use local civil time and the existing timezone-aware alignment machinery;
- preserve repeated hours and missing hours across DST transitions;
- reject unknown presets, periods, currencies, and incompatible overrides;
- expose a stable period code/label array as well as price arrays;
- record preset identifier, version/vintage, source URL, timezone, cycle,
  currency, and a deterministic resolved-schedule hash; and
- never claim exact half-hour boundaries on hourly data. Require a compatible
  resolution or emit an explicit approximation with provenance.

The no-tariff/flat-price path must reproduce 0.5.1 valuation bit-for-bit.

### Price-weighted valuation belongs in the year loop

`breos.runners.app.run_app_simulation()` replays a full timestep simulation for
each project year but currently reduces it to annual import/export energy before
economics runs. TOU import cost and export revenue must therefore be aggregated
inside that existing loop:

```text
timestep import/export energy
            +
resolved import/export price arrays
            |
            v
year-specific import cost, export revenue, and fixed charge
            |
            v
currency-neutral project cashflows and metrics
```

Do not retain every year's timestep frame merely to price it later. Preserve
annual energy totals alongside the new annual monetary components so energy and
money remain independently auditable. The no-system baseline must use the same
price arrays and calendar treatment.

### Smart charging is an instruction layer

`breos.battery._dispatch_dc_step()` and `simulate_energy_balance()` remain the
single owners of energy conservation, power limits, battery state, degradation,
replacement, and ledger construction. A strategy/controller prepares an
instruction stream; it does not simulate the battery itself.

A minimal per-step instruction can express:

- whether battery discharge is allowed;
- the minimum/reserve state of charge for discharge; and
- a grid-charge target or no grid charge.

The canonical step then applies that instruction while enforcing all physical
constraints. A zero-action instruction must be exactly equivalent to current
greedy self-consumption.

For DC-coupled systems, grid charging is an AC-to-DC path. Its conversion
efficiency must be explicit (for example `grid_charge_efficiency`) rather than
silently reusing the DC-to-AC inverter efficiency. The implementation must also
prevent grid charging while surplus PV is being exported unless a later,
explicitly documented strategy requires otherwise.

### Energy origin remains auditable

BREOS already tracks stored PV-origin energy. Grid charging must extend that
ledger rather than erase its meaning:

- PV charging increases stored energy and PV-origin stored energy;
- grid charging increases stored energy but not PV-origin stored energy;
- discharge is split between PV-origin and grid-origin energy by a documented
  allocation rule; and
- self-consumption and avoided-grid-emissions metrics count only eligible
  PV-origin energy.

At minimum results must report grid-charge energy and cost, battery discharge
split by origin, and final stored energy split by origin. Time-shifted grid
energy must not be advertised as avoided emissions while only a scalar annual
grid factor exists.

### Project boundaries are physical, not accounting tricks

BREOS already carries stored energy and degradation state from one simulated
year into the next. Retain that physical continuity. Optimisation and oracle
runs must additionally declare a terminal convention, such as cyclic SOC,
minimum terminal SOC, or explicit terminal-energy valuation, so free end-of-run
depletion cannot improve an objective.

Terminal-health sensitivity is useful reporting, but the dirty legacy
experiment should not be copied into dispatch objectives without being frozen,
reviewed, and justified independently.

## Proposed public configuration contract

The exact spelling should be validated against the declarative registry before
implementation, but the contract should look like this:

```toml
[tariff]
preset = "pt_2026_tri_daily"

[smart_charging]
mode = "fixed_target"          # disabled | fixed_target | daily_persistence
target_soc = 0.50
charge_periods = ["off_peak"]
discharge_periods = ["mid_peak", "peak"]
grid_charge_efficiency = 0.95
```

Required semantics:

- omitted `[smart_charging]` means today's greedy self-consumption;
- a preset may be overridden only through validated, documented fields;
- custom schedules and prices declare timezone and currency explicitly;
- fixed-target charging aims for a fraction of the usable SOC window, not a
  fraction of nominal capacity below the minimum SOC;
- period-based discharge policies are names, not hidden numeric codes;
- daily persistence declares its information set and warm-start policy; and
- configuration, resolved assumptions, and exported results contain enough
  provenance to reproduce the run.

`daily_persistence` may use the previous observed day's load/PV pattern and the
known tariff calendar to choose today's target. Day one must use a configured
target or no-grid-charge warm start. The legacy shortcut of seeding a repeated
TMY run with day-one truth is not causal and must not appear in the public
strategy.

## Tariff preset provenance

Presets are versioned data, not timeless country facts. The initial catalog
should be deliberately small.

### Portugal

The Portuguese 2026 daily/weekly bi- and tri-hourly periods and the approved
2027 period reform are suitable schedule candidates. Use ERSE's current tariff
page and published explainer as the primary sources:

- [ERSE electricity tariffs and prices](https://www.erse.pt/atividade/regulacao/tarifas-e-precos-eletricidade?filtro=Per%C3%ADodo+Regula%C3%A7%C3%A3o+2026-2029)
- [ERSE explainer for the approved 2027 schedules](https://www.erse.pt/media/3eec14ln/cp137_fecho_ersexplica_pr_horarios.pdf)

The legacy `PortugueseTOUCycle2027` schedule matches the approved half-hour
boundaries, but its docstring cites “Diretiva ERSE n.º 3/2026” as the source.
[ERSE's acts catalog](https://www.erse.pt/biblioteca/atos-e-documentos-da-erse/?tipologia=Atos+normativos)
identifies that directive as a gas-tariff act, not the electricity-period
decision. Correct the citation rather than copying it. The earlier CP137
proposal should remain a research fixture, not a public current preset.

Supplier prices must have their own source and effective dates; the period
schedule alone does not establish a retail price preset.

### Spain

The 2.0TD P1/P2/P3 period schedule is suitable, using Spain's official
[Circular 3/2020](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2020-1066&p=20200729&tn=1)
as the schedule source. The legacy code instead names Royal Decree 446/2023,
which concerns PVPC price calculation rather than originating the 2.0TD period
schedule. Port the schedule with the corrected source. Do not ship the legacy
estimated price values as official defaults without a dated primary source.

### Germany

Do not ship `GermanTOUCycle` as a generic official German preset. Under the
[Bundesnetzagentur §14a framework](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/Energie/SteuerbareVBE/artikel.html?nn=877500),
Module 3 has multiple price levels but the applicable windows and prices are
defined by the local network operator. Support user/provider-specific schedules
instead. A median-DSO research scenario could be bundled later only if labeled
as representative, with its full derivation and dataset provenance.

## Smart-charging modes for 0.7.0

### Supported: fixed target

During configured cheap periods, charge from the grid toward a target within
the usable SOC window, subject to battery charge power, grid/interface limits,
conversion efficiency, capacity fade, and replacement state. Discharge only in
configured periods. This is the production baseline because it is deterministic
and can be explained without forecasting claims.

### Experimental: causal daily persistence

Port the concept behind `solve_causal_persistence_schedule()` rather than its
standalone simulator. It repeats or derives a target from the previous observed
day over a finite horizon while using known future tariff periods. Its actions
must be replayed through the canonical BREOS simulation.

Legacy evidence in `docs/a2_daily_sc_oracle_handover.md` found that the offline
perfect schedule was positive in 54/54 examined cases with a median benefit of
about EUR 319, while the causal persistence schedule was positive in 30/54 with
a median benefit of about EUR 23. These are research results, not product
guarantees. They argue for an experimental label, explicit baseline comparison,
and avoiding the word “optimal.”

### Tools only: perfect-information oracles

Port two small validation tools after the runtime contract is stable:

- a daily target dynamic program based on `solve_daily_sc_schedule()`; and
- the cyclic-SOC LP bound from `compute_a2_perfect_foresight_bound.py`.

They should consume public/resolved BREOS inputs and replay their schedules
through production physics. They may establish upper bounds and regression
expectations, but must not become `App` strategies or share a duplicate battery
kernel.

## ACC recommendation

ACC should be split into an allocation core and a later sizing workflow. The
allocation core is much closer to being reusable than the original disposition
suggested:

- fixed coefficients;
- different fixed coefficients by local-time period;
- proportional-to-consumption coefficients; and
- community and per-participant energy metrics.

These are pure transformations over a community production series and a
`(timestep, participant)` load matrix. They can be ported into a focused module,
tentatively `breos.community`, without copying the old runner. The result needs
an explicit settlement ledger: energy offered for sharing, energy allocated,
participant consumption, residual community surplus, and grid import/export
must reconcile at every 15-minute settlement interval.

The reason not to put ACC on the 0.7.0 critical path is architectural rather
than a lack of value:

- `App` currently represents one site and one load, not a community with stable
  participant identities and per-member results;
- the ordering of shared PV, a community battery, behind-the-meter batteries,
  and grid settlement must be specified;
- participant-specific tariffs, ownership shares, and cost allocation cannot be
  inferred from the physical sharing coefficient;
- the legacy core has no focused ACC test suite; and
- `acc_sizer.py` fetches weather internally, bypasses the facade, assumes a
  550 W module and linear EUR/kWp capex, and explicitly ignores inverter
  clipping.

For 0.7.x, support fixed and proportional allocation first. E-REDES currently
lists fixed, proportional, hierarchical, and dynamic models, while describing
hierarchical and dynamic operation through its project-pilot workflow:

- [E-REDES sharing coefficients](https://www.e-redes.pt/pt-pt/coeficientes-de-partilha-0)
- [E-REDES sharing models](https://www.e-redes.pt/pt-pt/modelos-de-partilha-de-energia-em-autoconsumo)

The legacy quota-balancing algorithm can then ship as experimental after it has
a plain NumPy reference, focused invariants, and a mapping to an accepted
operator data-exchange contract. It must not be presented as the one official
Portuguese dynamic algorithm merely because it was used in a research case.

ACC sizing should eventually use the ordinary BREOS runner/sweep and the TOU
cashflow domain. Do not preserve `run_acc_sizer_logic()` as a separate
simulation stack.

## Numba for optimisation

Numba is a good fit for the repeated numerical objective evaluation beneath a
sweep, multistage search, or NSGA-II run. It does not accelerate the optimiser's
decision logic by itself. The largest gains should come from combining:

1. cached PV, load, and resolved-tariff arrays;
2. exact-equivalence deduplication of candidate configurations;
3. a JIT-compiled within-day dispatch loop; and
4. bounded parallel evaluation of independent candidates.

Do not revive the removed `breos.numba_kernels` module. Its whole-project
kernels duplicated degradation and replacement science and were not on a
production path; 0.6.0 deletes them.

0.6.0 already ships the day-kernel shape this section anticipated. It adds a
summary-only simulation path and the private `breos._numba_dispatch` daily
dispatch kernel, selectable for Monte Carlo through
`[montecarlo].execution_backend`, with Python retaining degradation, rainflow,
replacement, year-to-year state, and ledger assembly. See
[0.6x-projected-optimization-plan.md](0.6x-projected-optimization-plan.md).

0.7.x therefore *extends* that kernel rather than introducing one: strategy
preparation, resolved-tariff instruction arrays, and backend selection for
`App` and for projected optimization. Do not grow a second engine beside it.

The preferred implementation is one canonical day-kernel contract that accepts
resolved instruction arrays. Greedy and fixed-target TOU dispatch should use
the same kernel rather than growing separate `canonical_*` and `tou_*` engines.
A retained pure-Python reference must exercise the identical contract, and the
Numba import must stay lazy so normal `breos` import and non-optimisation runs do
not pay JIT startup cost.

Parallelism needs one owner. Use the serial day kernel inside process-parallel
candidate evaluation, or a batch `prange` kernel in a single process; do not
nested-parallelise both and oversubscribe CPUs. Since JIT compilation has a
fixed cost, enable the accelerated evaluator for sufficiently large batches,
not automatically for every one-off `App.run()`.

The performance PR needs a benchmark gate measured after warm-up, including
hourly and 15-minute TOU cases. It lands only if it produces a material
end-to-end optimisation speedup while matching the Python ledger, annual
summaries, degradation state, replacement events, and objective values within
declared tolerances.

## Companion economics work worth adding

The most valuable nonthermal companion from `pvbat/payback.py` and
`docs/adr/0003-real-economics-basis.md` is an explicit real-cashflow model:

- separate general inflation, electricity import escalation, export/feed-in
  escalation, O&M escalation, and replacement-capex learning;
- expose a documented real discount rate (or a precise nominal-to-real
  conversion) rather than mixing bases;
- retain component cashflows for initial capex, imports, exports, fixed charge,
  O&M, and replacements;
- distinguish simple payback from discounted payback; and
- define sustained payback so a temporarily positive cumulative balance that
  later falls negative is not reported as final payback.

New fields should be currency-neutral, with existing `*_eur` fields retained as
compatibility aliases only when the resolved currency is EUR. This work aligns
with ROADMAP's currency and economic-scenario sequencing and should be designed
with TOU rather than retrofitted afterward.

Two smaller additions are also in scope if capacity permits:

- tariff-period energy/cost diagnostics and a comparison recipe implemented via
  the existing sweep workflow; and
- explicit terminal-energy and terminal-health sensitivity outputs for research
  comparisons, without altering default dispatch.

## Delivery sequence

Keep each implementation PR reviewable and preserve the `breos.App` facade:

1. **Freeze and specify.** Record source blobs, write the tariff/strategy ADR,
   settle configuration names, and correct regulatory provenance.
2. **Tariff domain.** Add currency, schedules, price resolution, packaged data,
   validation, and flat-path compatibility tests without changing dispatch.
3. **TOU valuation.** Aggregate price-weighted annual costs/revenues in the App
   runner and add currency-neutral component cashflows.
4. **Dispatch seam.** Introduce no-op instructions around the canonical battery
   step and prove exact old-path parity for native and BLAST degradation.
5. **Accelerated evaluator.** Extend the existing private 0.6.0 day kernel to
   the optimisation and `App` paths, and to resolved-tariff instruction
   arrays, only if warm benchmarks and full parity tests pass.
6. **Fixed-target strategy.** Add AC-to-DC grid charging, origin accounting,
   provenance, and terminal-state reporting.
7. **Persistence and oracles.** Add the experimental causal controller, then
   the offline DP and LP validation tools.
8. **Workflow integration.** Add App/CLI examples, dotted sweep support,
   tariff-comparison output, documentation, and explicit handling for
   unsupported Monte Carlo combinations.
9. **ACC follow-up.** Define the participant/settlement contract, port fixed
   and proportional allocation, and leave dynamic allocation experimental.

The first three steps can land without enabling smart charging. Step 4 must not
change default results. Steps 5-7 are opt-in. Step 9 belongs in 0.7.x and need
not hold the 0.7.0 release unless the participant contract is settled early.

## Acceptance criteria

### Tariff resolution and valuation

- Flat/no-tariff results match the pre-port results bit-for-bit.
- PT 2026, PT 2027, and ES 2.0TD boundaries have table-driven tests against
  primary sources.
- A 23-hour spring day and 25-hour autumn day are priced without inventing,
  dropping, or double-counting energy.
- Two timezone representations of the same instant resolve consistently.
- Half-hour transitions are exact at 30/15-minute resolution and rejected or
  explicitly approximated at hourly resolution.
- Unknown presets, periods, currencies, and overrides fail with actionable
  messages; there is no country fallback.
- Import cost, export revenue, and standing charge reconcile exactly with the
  timestep ledger for every project year and for the no-system baseline.
- Mixed-currency inputs cannot silently produce one NPV.

### Dispatch and battery lifecycle

- Disabled smart charging is exactly the current greedy dispatch.
- Every step conserves energy across PV, load, grid, battery, conversion loss,
  and curtailment.
- Grid charging respects usable capacity, SOC limits, charge power, grid limits,
  conversion efficiency, degradation, and replacements.
- Grid charging never coincides with unconsumed PV export under the initial
  strategy contract.
- PV-origin and grid-origin energy reconcile through charge, discharge,
  year-to-year carry, capacity fade, and replacement.
- Both native and BLAST degradation see smart-charging throughput through the
  same production lifecycle.
- Terminal SOC/energy convention and initial/final state are recorded.

### Controller integrity

- Changing future load/PV truth cannot change an earlier causal-persistence
  action.
- Day-one warm start contains no future truth.
- Oracle schedules are replayed through production physics before benefits are
  reported.
- Perfect-information outputs are labeled as bounds, never forecasts or
  deployable strategies.
- Results record controller mode, parameters, tariff provenance, schedule hash,
  grid-charge energy/cost, and terminal convention.

### Accelerated evaluation

- The Python and Numba day paths match for greedy and fixed-target dispatch at
  hourly and 15-minute resolution.
- Ledger columns, annual summaries, degradation state, replacement events, and
  objective values are parity-tested, not only terminal SOC.
- A warm end-to-end optimisation benchmark records candidate count, hardware,
  wall time, peak memory, and speedup.
- Process and Numba thread counts are bounded; nested oversubscription is
  prevented.
- One-off runs retain a no-JIT path and importing BREOS does not import Numba.

### ACC follow-up

- Every interval reconciles production, allocations, participant consumption,
  grid flows, and community surplus.
- Coefficients satisfy range/sum rules, including zero-load and excess-PV cases.
- Fixed and proportional results are tested at the required 15-minute
  settlement resolution and across DST transitions.
- Participant identity and result ordering are stable under serialization.
- Community batteries and participant batteries are rejected until their
  settlement ordering is explicitly implemented.
- The NumPy dynamic-allocation reference and optional Numba kernel have exact
  invariant/parity tests before the experimental mode is exposed.

### Public workflows

- `breos.App`, CLI validation, examples, serialization, and sweep results expose
  the same resolved contract.
- Pure tariff revaluation can reuse energy results only for price-blind
  dispatch; a price-aware strategy forces re-simulation.
- Unsupported Monte Carlo/optimisation combinations fail explicitly rather than
  silently ignoring smart charging.
- User documentation distinguishes example presets from project-specific
  supplier offers and includes sources/effective dates.
- No thermal module, field, dependency, fixture, configuration, or output is
  introduced by these ports.

## Deferred questions

Resolve these in the strategy ADR, not ad hoc in implementation:

- whether daily persistence is public-experimental in 0.7.0 or remains a
  research tool until 0.7.1;
- whether the controller instruction is an array, iterator, or typed protocol;
- the allocation rule when discharging mixed PV/grid-origin stored energy;
- the correct grid/interface power cap for AC-to-DC charging in each topology;
- whether hourly input with half-hour tariff transitions is rejected by default
  or allowed only with an explicit approximation flag; and
- the ledger schema/version change needed for origin-split discharge and
  component cashflows.

None of these questions requires reopening the thermal-storage exclusion.
